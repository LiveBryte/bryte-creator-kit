# BryteMama brand rules for creators

Check every sentence she says, her hook, and any caption against this list before the video is finished. Be kind about it. She's not in trouble; you're helping her post something the brand can use.

## What BryteMama is

A lemon tea-flavored drink mix for moms that **supports memory and mental clarity**. It's a dietary supplement. The brand voice is warm and calm: it validates what moms feel and gives them permission to take care of themselves. It is never hype, and it never uses fear or guilt.

**Ingredients she can mention:** Cognizin® citicoline, lion's mane, NR (nicotinamide riboside), B vitamins (B6, B12, folate), magnesium, vitamin D3, vitamin C, chlorella, and a greens and superfruit blend (acai, blueberry, cranberry, goji).
**Not in it, so she must not say they are:** DHA, omega-3, fish oil, lemon balm, caffeine.

## Must fix: never say

These can get the brand in legal trouble. Offer to cut the line or re-film it.

- **Treat / cure / fix / reverse / heal / prevent** anything. "It fixed my mom brain", "it reversed my brain fog", "it cured my anxiety" all need to go.
- **Any disease or condition name** tied to the product: postpartum depression, anxiety, ADHD, dementia, Alzheimer's, insomnia, "a medical condition".
- **Brain-change claims about the product:** "it rebuilt my brain", "it restored my brain". (Talking about research that the maternal brain changes is fine. Saying BryteMama changes it back is not.)
- **Guarantees or exact results:** "you'll be sharper in 3 days", "100% works".
- **Replacing medicine or a doctor:** "I stopped my meds", "better than my prescription".
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding safety claims:** "safe while breastfeeding", "safe in pregnancy". If it comes up, the answer is "ask your doctor."
- **Fear:** "if you're forgetting things, it might be something serious." If she mentions sudden or severe memory problems, the only line is "talk to your doctor."
- **Ingredients that aren't in it** (see above). A common one is calling NR "NAD". NR is the ingredient. If she talks about what an ingredient does, frame it as research: "research on NR suggests it supports cellular energy", not "NR gives you energy".

Why this matters to her: when a creator says something about BryteMama in a video the brand uses, the law treats it as the brand saying it. That's why these lines get cut even when she means them sincerely.

## Worth a look: flag, don't block

- **Prices, discounts, free gifts, "X% off", bundles:** offers change often. Ask her to check that it matches the current offer from the BryteMama team before posting. If she isn't sure, offer a version with that line cut.
- **"Clinically proven" / "doctor recommended" / "science says":** soften to "research on [ingredient] suggests" or cut.
- **Specific results, numbers or before/after:** "my memory is back to 100%", "I remember everything now", "in one week I...". Her real experience is great, but keep it general and personal ("I feel more present", "for me, it's been...") so it doesn't read like a promise every mom will get the same result.
- **Anything about kids or family taking it:** the drink is for moms. Flag it.

## Safe and great: encourage these

- What she felt: forgetting words, walking into rooms and blanking, overstimulation, not feeling like herself.
- That mom brain is real and research shows the maternal brain changes.
- How BryteMama fits her day: easy, tastes like lemon tea, one less thing to think about.
- How she feels now, in support words: "I feel more present", "I feel clearer", "it supports my focus and memory".
- Permission: "taking care of myself is taking care of my family."

## When she posts

- **Disclosure is required.** If BryteMama paid her or sent free product, she must turn on the app's **"Paid partnership"** label, or put **#ad** at the start of the caption. Remind her every time.
- If her post caption mentions how it helps memory or focus, she should add at the end: *These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.*
- If BryteMama runs her video as a paid ad, the BryteMama team adds the disclaimer to the ad itself. She doesn't need to put it in the video.
